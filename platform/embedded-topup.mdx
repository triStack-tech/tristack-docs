title: "Top up from your own admin console"
description: "Put wallet top-up inside your own product, under your own name and your own domain: mint the session on your server, open it on a click, read the result without trusting the browser."
---

Your own admin console can take a top-up, instead of sending your operators to the Tristack
dashboard. Your backend mints a short-lived permit, your page opens a window with it, and the
money lands in the same account wallet every product spends from.

The window can carry **your** name, **your** logo and **your** domain, so the people who use
it see the console they already work in.
[Put your own name on it →](#your-own-name-on-the-window)
[Wallet and billing →](/platform/wallet)

<Info>
  Two credentials, two audiences. `POST /v1/wallet/topup-sessions` and `GET /v1/wallet`
  answer to a project API key and belong on your **backend**. The window itself carries a
  session permit, and that permit is the only thing a browser ever holds.
</Info>

## Why your server mints the permit

Only your backend knows which of your signed-in people may spend your company's money. The
support agent who may read the balance is not the finance lead who may add to it, and that
line lives in your product, in your roles table, behind your session cookie. Tristack cannot
see it and does not try to guess: your server vouches for the payer by minting a permit, and
the browser presents nothing else.

Which is why the permit is deliberately small:

- **Single use.** It opens exactly one payment. A second attempt on the same URL is refused.
- **Fifteen minutes** from mint to the moment an order is created.
- **Carried in the URL fragment**, not the query string. A fragment is never sent to a
  server, so the permit stays out of our access logs, out of any proxy's, and out of the
  `Referer` header on anything the page links to.
- **Narrow.** The hosted page reads the account name, the balance, the minimum top-up and
  the account owner's email address, which is used to prefill the payment form. It cannot
  reach the ledger, the top-up history, your API keys, or anything else on the account.

<Warning>
  **`token` is returned once and stored only as a hash**, exactly like an API key. No screen,
  no endpoint and nobody at Tristack Technologies LLP can show it to you again, which is
  fine: it is meant to reach one browser and expire.

  Your `tsk_live_…` key is the credential that can mint permits at all, so it stays on the
  server. A key in a browser bundle is a key somebody else can spend from.
  [Projects and keys →](/platform/projects-and-keys)
</Warning>

## Setup, once per project

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch wallet top-up on for the project" icon="toggle-on">
    Open the project in the dashboard and turn on **Allow this project's keys to open the
    top-up window**. A key on a project without it authenticates correctly and is then
    refused with `403 wallet_topup_disabled`, before any session exists.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add at least one allowed origin" icon="globe">
    The exact origin your page opens the window from: scheme, host and port, and nothing
    else. Up to 10 per project.

    | Written like this | Accepted | Why |
    |---|:---:|---|
    | `https://app.example.com` | Yes | Scheme and host, no path |
    | `https://app.example.com:8443` | Yes | A non-default port is part of the origin |
    | `http://localhost:3000` | Yes | Plain `http` is allowed for loopback, which is where the integration gets written |
    | `https://app.example.com/wallet` | No | A path is not part of an origin. Store the origin here, put the path in `returnUrl` |
    | `http://app.example.com` | No | Plain `http` in production would put the permit, and the balance that comes back, on the wire in the clear |
    | `https://*.example.com` | No | There are no wildcards. List each origin you actually use |

    Stored and compared exactly: lowercased, no trailing slash. A mismatch answers
    `403 origin_not_allowed`, and the usual cause is a forgotten dashboard entry rather than
    a typo in the code.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Both are required, and the origin is not optional decoration.** With no origin on the
  list, minting is refused however the switch is set. The window reports its outcome by
  posting a message to one exact origin, and that message carries your balance. With nothing
  recorded there is nowhere safe to send it, and the alternative (`postMessage(payload, "*")`)
  hands the balance to whatever page ends up holding the window.
</Note>

One more gate worth knowing before you wire this into a checkout: the **account owner's email
must be verified**, or minting answers `403 account_owner_unverified`. It is the API-key
equivalent of the verified-email check the dashboard applies before it takes a payment.

## Your own name on the window

The window is for **your own people**: your operators, topping up your own wallet, from your
own admin screen. So it can carry your name rather than ours. Pass `brandName` and
`brandLogoUrl` when you mint, and both the window and the Razorpay payment sheet show your
brand.

With `brandName`, `brandLogoUrl` and a hostname of your own, an operator opening this window
sees your logo, your name and your domain, and never leaves your console.

Three things still say Tristack, and none of them is a setting we can flip:

- **The card statement.** We are the merchant of record: the money is paid to us, and lands in
  your wallet. A statement line naming you would be false, and banks treat that as a
  descriptor violation rather than a branding choice.
- **"Secured by Razorpay" on the payment sheet.** It is their sheet, and that line is how a
  payer knows the card form is not a page you drew.
- **The receipt email**, sent to the account owner's address once the balance actually moves.
  It is our record of money we received, and it goes to your billing contact, not to whoever
  happened to be at the keyboard.

<Warning>
  Do not put your brand on this window for anyone who is not you: not your own customers, not
  a third party paying on your behalf. The payment goes to Tristack, and dressing that screen
  in your name for someone whose money it is would misrepresent who is being paid. Between you
  and your own staff, there is nobody to mislead: they know whose wallet they are filling.
</Warning>

### Serving it from your own domain

The window is a page on `api.tristack.tech`, so by default that is what the address bar reads.
To serve it from `pay.example.com` instead:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tell us the hostname" icon="envelope">
    Write to [support@tristack.tech](mailto:support@tristack.tech) with the project and the
    hostname you want. We add it, and reply with the exact CNAME target and the validation
    record to create. It is not self-serve: a certificate has to be issued for your hostname,
    and that cannot happen from an API call alone.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add the records we send back" icon="server">
    Two records on your domain:

    | Type | Name | Value |
    |---|---|---|
    | `CNAME` | `pay` | the target we send you |
    | `TXT` | the validation name we send you | the validation value we send you |

    The `TXT` record proves to the certificate authority that you control the name. Leave both
    in place: the `CNAME` is how traffic arrives, and the `TXT` is re-checked at every renewal,
    every 90 days. Deleting it after issue works until the day it silently does not.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Point the origin at it" icon="link">
    Add `https://pay.example.com` to the project's allowed origins, and mint as usual. The URL
    that comes back is on your hostname, so the address bar reads your domain.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Nothing to register with Razorpay.** Checkout opens from a key and an order id and reports
  back into a JavaScript callback; there is no allowed-domain list to keep, and no per-URL
  registration whatever hostname the page is served from. The only work is the certificate,
  and that is ours.
</Note>

## The whole flow

### 1. Mint the session on your server

This is the call that decides someone may spend the wallet, so it runs where you already know
who is signed in, with the API key that never leaves your servers.

<CodeGroup>
```js Node
// Your backend. `req.user` is your session, not ours: this is the moment you decide
// that this particular person is allowed to spend the company's money.
if (!req.user.canTopUpWallet) return res.status(403).end();

const mint = await fetch("https://api.tristack.tech/v1/wallet/topup-sessions", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: {
    Authorization: `Bearer ${process.env.TRISTACK_API_KEY}`,
    "Content-Type": "application/json",
  },
  body: JSON.stringify({
    origin: "https://app.example.com",
    amountPaise: 50000,          // Rs 500. Omit this to let the payer choose.
    reference: "invoice 4471",   // your own label, shown on the window
  }),
});

const { url, expiresAt } = await mint.json();
// Hand `url` to that browser and nowhere else. Do not log it, do not email it.
```

```bash curl
curl -X POST https://api.tristack.tech/v1/wallet/topup-sessions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "origin": "https://app.example.com",
    "amountPaise": 50000,
    "reference": "invoice 4471"
  }'
```
</CodeGroup>

<ParamField body="origin" type="string" required>
  The origin your page will open the window from, and it must already be on the project's
  list. You send it rather than us reading an `Origin` header, because this call comes from a
  server, which has no meaningful one.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="amountPaise" type="integer">
  A preset amount in **paise**, so Rs 500 is `50000` and the Rs 100 minimum is `10000`.
  Preset it and the payer cannot change it. Omit it and they choose on the window.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="mode" type="string" default="popup">
  `popup` or `redirect`. See [redirect mode](#redirect-mode-for-mobile-webviews).
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="returnUrl" type="string">
  Required in redirect mode, ignored in popup mode. Must be on `origin`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="brandName" type="string">
  Your own name, shown to the payer instead of ours: on the window, and on the payment sheet
  itself. Up to 64 characters. Omit it and the window shows your account name and the sheet
  shows ours.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="brandLogoUrl" type="string">
  An **https** URL for your logo, shown on the window and the payment sheet. Anything else is
  refused with `invalid_brand_logo`: a logo is fetched by the payer's browser on a payment
  screen, and an `http` one is a mixed-content warning exactly where a warning does most harm.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="reference" type="string">
  Your own label for this top-up, up to 128 characters, shown to the payer so they can see
  which of your systems asked for it.
</ParamField>

```json 200 OK
{
  "sessionId": "5c9b0f22-9d51-4a2e-9e4f-0d6f3a71b2c8",
  "token": "tsx_9tR2mVxK7pQ0aZ4bN6cD1eF3gH5jL8sW2yU4iO6kP0m",
  "url": "https://api.tristack.tech/topup.html#tsx=tsx_9tR2mVxK7pQ0aZ4bN6cD1eF3gH5jL8sW2yU4iO6kP0m",
  "expiresAt": "2026-08-21T09:27:44Z"
}
```

`url` already carries `token` in its fragment, so hand the frontend `url` and forget `token`
exists. When the mint is refused, nothing was created and nothing was charged:

| Status | `code` | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| `403` | `wallet_topup_disabled` | The project's top-up switch is off |
| `403` | `origin_not_allowed` | `origin` is not on the project's list, or is not a usable origin |
| `403` | `account_owner_unverified` | The account owner's email is not verified yet |
| `400` | `invalid_return_url` | Redirect mode with no `returnUrl`, or one that is not on `origin` |
| `invalid_brand_logo` | `brandLogoUrl` was not an `https` URL | Serve the logo over `https`. A payment screen is the worst place for a mixed-content warning |
| `400` | `below_minimum_topup` | A preset `amountPaise` under `10000` (Rs 100) |
| `503` | `razorpay_disabled` | Payments are not configured on this deployment |

### 2. Open the window from the click

```js Your frontend
let topUpWindow = null;

document.querySelector("#add-credit").addEventListener("click", async () => {
  // window.open has to run INSIDE the click handler. A browser allows a popup only
  // while a user gesture is still the reason the code is running, and an `await` ends
  // that: open the window empty now, point it at the session URL when it arrives.
  topUpWindow = window.open("about:blank", "tristack-topup", "width=460,height=680");

  // null means the popup was blocked anyway, which is normal in a mobile webview.
  if (!topUpWindow) return startRedirectTopUp();

  // Your own endpoint, which mints the session with your API key.
  const { url } = await fetch("/internal/topup-session", { method: "POST" }).then((r) => r.json());
  topUpWindow.location = url;
});

window.addEventListener("message", (event) => {
  // All three checks are required. Any page can post a message to yours, and this
  // message carries a balance: skipping one means trusting a number a stranger made up.
  if (event.origin !== "https://api.tristack.tech") return; // 1. who sent it
  if (event.source !== topUpWindow) return;                 // 2. the window you opened
  if (event.data?.source !== "tristack") return;            // 3. ours, not other traffic on your page

  const { status, balancePaise, message } = event.data;

  switch (status) {
    case "credited":
      // Done. balancePaise is the balance after the credit.
      showBalance(balancePaise);
      break;
    case "pending":
      // The money WAS taken and lands within minutes. Never offer to pay again here.
      showNotice("Payment received. Your balance updates in a few minutes.");
      break;
    case "cancelled":
      // The payer dismissed the payment form. Nothing was charged.
      showNotice(message);
      break;
  }

  refreshBalance(); // your backend, calling GET /v1/wallet
});
```

<ResponseField name="source" type="string">
  Always `"tristack"`. The marker that separates this from every other `postMessage` on your
  page: analytics tags, embeds, browser extensions.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="type" type="string">
  Always `"wallet.topup"`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="status" type="string">
  `credited`, `pending` or `cancelled`. Branch on this.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="balancePaise" type="integer | null">
  The balance in paise as Tristack read it when the message was sent, or null if the payment
  never got far enough to read one. On `pending` it is the balance **before** the credit
  settles, so treat it as stale.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="message" type="string">
  A sentence written for the payer. Safe to display, never safe to branch on.
</ResponseField>

The window does not close itself. It shows the payer what happened and leaves a Close
button, so a result stays readable rather than vanishing at the moment it matters.

`cancelled` means the payer dismissed the **payment form** while the window was still open.
It does not mean they closed the window: a closed window runs no script, so it posts nothing
at all. Treat "no message" as a real outcome, not an impossible one, and let your next
balance read settle the question rather than the message.

<Warning>
  **`pending` means the money was taken.** It is not a failure and it is not "try again". The
  payment succeeded at the bank; Tristack has not finished writing it to the wallet yet.
  Three independent paths lead to the same credit (the browser confirmation, the payment
  webhook, and an hourly sweep), and one of them lands within minutes.

  Show "payment received, balance updating" and refresh from `GET /v1/wallet`. **Never
  present a pay-again button on a `pending`.** That is precisely how somebody pays twice, and
  the second charge is real money that then has to be refunded by hand.
</Warning>

## Redirect mode, for mobile webviews

Inside most mobile webviews there is no popup to open and no opener to post a message back
to. Same session mint, two extra fields:

```bash Mint a redirect session
curl -X POST https://api.tristack.tech/v1/wallet/topup-sessions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "origin": "https://app.example.com",
    "mode": "redirect",
    "returnUrl": "https://app.example.com/wallet"
  }'
```

`returnUrl` is required here and must sit on `origin`. That binding is what stops the return
URL becoming an open redirect wearing a Tristack address, and `400 invalid_return_url` is the
answer when it does not hold.

Send the browser to `url` directly (`window.location.href = url`), with no popup and no
message listener. When the payment ends, the browser comes back to your page with one query
parameter appended:

```text
https://app.example.com/wallet?tristack_topup=credited
```

`tristack_topup` carries the same three values as `status`.

<Warning>
  **`tristack_topup` is a hint for your loading state and nothing else.** Confirm with
  `GET /v1/wallet` before you show a number, unlock a feature or write anything down. Anything
  in a URL the user's own browser carried is something the user can rewrite, and
  `?tristack_topup=credited` is four seconds of typing.
</Warning>

## The balance is Tristack's to know

```bash Refresh your cache
curl https://api.tristack.tech/v1/wallet \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY"
```

```json 200 OK
{
  "balancePaise": 47500,
  "minTopUpPaise": 10000,
  "lowBalance": false,
  "autoTopUpThresholdPaise": null,
  "autoTopUpState": "Disabled"
}
```

<ResponseField name="balancePaise" type="integer">
  The whole account's balance in paise. Rs 475 is `47500`. Every amount on this API is an
  integer in paise, in both directions.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="lowBalance" type="boolean">
  Measured against the account's auto top-up threshold when one is armed, and against
  `minTopUpPaise` otherwise, so every integration means the same thing by "low" instead of
  each inventing a number.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="autoTopUpState" type="string">
  `Disabled`, `AwaitingMandate`, `Active` or `Suspended`. Auto top-up itself is a dashboard
  feature, deliberately out of reach of an API key: a leaked key must not be able to put a
  card on file.
</ResponseField>

**Tristack holds the balance, and your copy is a cache.** Refresh it on a schedule, refresh
it after every top-up result, and store it as a display value with a timestamp beside it. An
app that keeps its own authoritative number ends up with two balances and no way to say which
is right, and it finds out at the worst possible moment: when someone is asking why a request
was refused while the screen still says there is money.

## When a session stops working

| Status | `code` | When | What your app does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `401` | `topup_session_invalid` | The permit is missing, unknown, or not one of ours | Mint a fresh session |
| `410` | `topup_session_expired` | Fifteen minutes passed without a payment, or a paid window sat open past its confirmation grace | Mint a fresh session |
| `410` | `topup_session_used` | It already opened an order. A reloaded window, or a stale URL out of browser history, lands here | Mint a fresh session |

All three mean exactly the same thing to your app, which is why they are worth handling in
one branch: the permit is spent, and the fix is a new one.

<Note>
  Your backend never sends the session header; the hosted page does. These three reach you as
  a window telling the payer it cannot continue, so the recovery is a fresh click on your own
  button, not a retry of the same URL. Do not cache a session URL, and do not put one in an
  email: it is a single-use permit with a fifteen-minute life, not a payment link.
</Note>
