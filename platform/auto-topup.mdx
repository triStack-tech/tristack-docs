Automatic top-ups are a standing instruction. When the wallet falls below a threshold you
set, Tristack refills it from a payment method you have saved, so a call is never refused at
three in the morning because nobody was watching the balance.
[Wallet and billing →](/platform/wallet)

Everything below is set up on the **Wallet** page in the dashboard, under your account
session. It is deliberately not on the API-key surface: a leaked key can already spend the
wallet, and it must not also be able to put a card on file.

## What it does

The balance is checked against your threshold every fifteen minutes. Below it, a refill cycle
starts and adds your refill amount to the wallet. Above it, nothing happens at all.

The threshold defaults to **Rs 100**, which is also the smallest the dashboard accepts, and
it is tunable up to Rs 100,000.

<Note>
  **The wallet page takes rupees.** All three amounts below are rupee fields: type `500` for
  Rs 500, not `50000`. The dashboard converts to paise before it saves, because the API
  underneath is integer paise like everything else on the platform, and you only meet those
  paise if you call it yourself. `PATCH /api/v1/wallet/auto-topup` takes `thresholdPaise`,
  `amountPaise` and `dailyCapPaise`; `GET /v1/wallet` reports `autoTopUpThresholdPaise`.
  [Money is always an integer in paise →](/platform/wallet)
</Note>

## A charge is never immediate

<Warning>
  **The money does not arrive the moment the threshold trips.** RBI's e-mandate framework
  requires the payer to be notified before every recurring debit, so the cycle is always
  threshold tripped, notice email, then the charge roughly **24 hours** later.

  **Size your threshold to cover about a day of your own spend.** This is the one number on
  the page that people get wrong. A threshold sized for an hour of traffic empties the wallet
  inside the notice window, and the refill then lands *after* the outage instead of before
  it, which is the exact failure this feature exists to prevent.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="The balance crosses the threshold" icon="arrow-trend-down">
    The check that finds it opens one cycle and records the balance that triggered it. At most
    one cycle per account is ever open, so a balance that keeps falling during the wait does
    not stack up a second notice or a second charge.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A notice email goes out" icon="envelope">
    Sent to the account owner. It names the amount, the last four digits of the payment
    method, and the earliest moment the charge can happen. **Nothing has been charged at this
    point.**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Roughly 24 hours pass" icon="clock">
    The wallet carries on being spent normally throughout. This is the window your threshold
    has to be big enough to survive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The charge is presented" icon="credit-card">
    The saved payment method is charged and the wallet is credited through the same path as a
    manual top-up, so it appears in the ledger as an ordinary `Topup` row. Recurring payments
    settle asynchronously, so the credit can land a few minutes after the charge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Sizing the threshold

Take what the account spends on a normal day and set the threshold at least that high, then
add headroom for a busy one. The refill amount can be generous; the threshold is the number
that decides whether you stay online.

| A normal day costs you | A threshold that survives the notice window |
|---|---|
| Rs 50 | Rs 100, the default |
| Rs 400 | Rs 600 |
| Rs 2,000 | Rs 3,000 |

Raising the threshold means raising the other two amounts with it. The refill has to be
**more** than the threshold, and it cannot exceed the daily cap, so a Rs 3,000 threshold
needs a refill above Rs 3,000 and a cap at least as large as that refill. Left at the
Rs 2,000 default cap, that arrangement will not save.

Remember that Manifold reserves `max_tokens` in full while a request is in flight, so the
balance a threshold has to outlast is the held one, not the settled one.
[How holds work →](/platform/wallet)

### What stands a charge down

Once a notice is out, three things cancel the charge before it happens. None of them counts
as a failure:

- **The balance recovers on its own.** A refund, a released hold, or simply less traffic than
  expected puts you back above the threshold, and the cycle stands down.
- **You top up by hand during the notice period.** Same thing: the balance is above the
  threshold when the charge comes due, so nothing is charged. This is the deliberate way to
  cancel a pending automatic charge you did not want.
- **You switch automatic top-ups off during the notice period.** The state is re-read at the
  moment of the charge, not at the moment of the notice.

<Note>
  **No notice, no charge.** If the deployment cannot send email, no cycle is started at all.
  There is no way to give the notice the rules require, and charging without it is not an
  option, so the balance is left alone rather than quietly debited.
</Note>

## Setting it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the wallet page" icon="wallet">
    Automatic top-ups live in their own panel there. Your email has to be verified before you
    can register a payment method, exactly as for a manual top-up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the three amounts" icon="sliders">
    Threshold, refill amount and daily cap. The refill amount has to be more than the
    threshold, and the dashboard refuses the combination if it is not.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete your billing profile" icon="id-card">
    Open **Profile** from the menu in the top right and fill in the billing details, an Indian
    mobile number among them. **The number is read from the profile**, not from a field on the
    wallet page: your bank will not authorise a standing instruction for a customer it has no
    number to reach, and taking it from anywhere else would let a mandate be authorised
    against a number the billing identity does not claim.

    An incomplete profile answers `403 billing_profile_incomplete`, and a complete one with no
    mobile number answers `400 invalid_contact`. A refill above Rs 50,000 needs a PAN on the
    profile as well, because the registration charge is that refill: without one it answers
    `403 pan_required`.
    [Billing profile →](/platform/billing-profile)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a payment method" icon="credit-card">
    Checkout opens and you authorise a mandate: a standing permission for Tristack to charge
    that instrument later, up to a ceiling, without you being present. Read the warning below
    before you click through this step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that it reads On" icon="circle-check">
    The panel then shows the last four digits of the instrument, the per-charge ceiling it was
    registered with, and when the mandate itself expires. Mandates are registered for five
    years, so re-registration is rare.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Registering a payment method is a real charge, not a token one.** Card networks will not
  authorise a mandate on a zero-value transaction, so the first refill doubles as the
  registration payment: your refill amount is taken then and there, and it credits your wallet
  like any other top-up.

  It is not Rs 1 that gets reversed, and it is not an extra charge on top of your first
  automatic refill. Expect that amount on your card statement dated the day you set this up.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Occasionally the payment succeeds and the bank returns no usable mandate. **The money is not
  lost**: it is credited to the wallet exactly like a manual top-up. Only the automatic part
  failed, so the panel stays on "setup unfinished" and you can register again or use a
  different card. Do not read that message as a failed payment.
</Note>

## The three amounts, and what each protects

| Amount | Default | Protects you against |
|---|---|---|
| **Threshold** | Rs 100 | Running out. It is the trigger point, and its real job is to cover the notice window |
| **Refill amount** | Rs 500 | Topping up too little to matter. It is what lands in the wallet each time, and what the registration charge costs |
| **Daily cap** | Rs 2,000 | A mistake. It is the ceiling on what automatic top-ups may spend in any rolling 24 hours |

Those are the figures you type on the wallet page. The same three defaults reach
`PATCH /api/v1/wallet/auto-topup` as `thresholdPaise: 10000`, `amountPaise: 50000` and
`dailyCapPaise: 200000`, because that is the surface where amounts are paise.

<Warning>
  **The refill amount must be more than the threshold.** Refill Rs 100 against a Rs 100
  threshold and the balance lands straight back in the trigger zone: the next check trips
  again, another notice goes out, and the card is charged over and over for a wallet that is
  never above water. The dashboard refuses that combination rather than letting you find it on
  your statement. A refill worth several times the threshold is the comfortable shape.
</Warning>

Every rule is checked when you save, so nothing here can be discovered later by a worker:

| Rule | Refused with |
|---|---|
| Threshold between Rs 100 and Rs 100,000 | `400 invalid_threshold` |
| Refill amount between Rs 100 and Rs 100,000 | `400 invalid_amount` |
| Refill amount more than the threshold | `400 amount_below_threshold` |
| Refill amount no larger than the daily cap | `400 amount_above_daily_cap` |
| Refill amount no larger than the ceiling on the saved payment method | `400 amount_above_mandate_limit` |

### About the daily cap

It counts only what automatic top-ups themselves have charged in the last 24 hours. Your own
manual top-ups do not count against it, and the window rolls rather than resetting at
midnight.

A refill that would breach the cap is refused outright rather than trimmed to fit, because a
part-refill would land back under the threshold and trigger again immediately. The wallet is
therefore allowed to run down and a manual top-up is the fix, which is the correct behaviour
for a limit that exists to bound a mistake.

The cap does one more thing: it is the per-charge ceiling written into the mandate at the card
network when you register. Raising the refill amount past a ceiling already burned in needs
the payment method added again, because raising the cap in the dashboard cannot move a limit
the network is holding.

## The four states

| Dashboard | State | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Off** | `Disabled` | The default, and where switching it off returns it. Any saved payment method is kept |
| **Setup unfinished** | `AwaitingMandate` | Asked for, no usable payment method yet: the registration payment was not completed, or it completed without the bank confirming a mandate. Nothing is watched and nothing is charged |
| **On** | `Active` | A confirmed payment method is on file and the balance is being watched. The only state that ever starts a cycle |
| **Stopped after failed charges** | `Suspended` | The platform stopped it after three charges failed in a row |

### Stopped after failed charges

**Three consecutive failed charges suspend automatic top-ups and send you an email.** Three
declines is a dead instrument rather than a bad afternoon, and a fourth attempt would only add
another line to your statement without adding a rupee to your wallet.

The two usual causes, in order:

- **The card expired.** Easily the most common, and invisible until the first charge after the
  expiry date.
- **The mandate was cancelled at the bank.** Someone revoked the standing instruction in the
  banking app, or the issuer reissued the card with a new number and did not carry the mandate
  across.

Any successful charge resets the streak to zero. Switching automatic top-ups back on also
clears it, deliberately: an arrangement you have looked at and re-armed on purpose deserves a
full set of attempts rather than one. If the instrument itself is dead, re-arming against it
simply fails again, so add the payment method afresh instead.

Suspended is kept separate from Off for one reason: you did not ask for it, so you have to be
told about it.

## Turning it off, and removing the payment method

Two different levers, and the difference matters:

| | Switch off | Remove the payment method |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Refills stop | Yes | Yes |
| Saved payment method | Kept | Forgotten by Tristack |
| Turning it back on later | One click | Register again, with a real charge again |
| A cycle already in its notice window | Stood down, nothing charged | Stood down, nothing charged |

<Warning>
  **Removing the payment method does not cancel the mandate at your bank.** It removes our
  reference to it, so Tristack can no longer present it and never will again. The mandate
  itself is an instruction registered at the card network, and only your card issuer can
  revoke it. Do that in your bank's app or by asking them, the same way you would end any
  other standing instruction.
</Warning>

Nothing here is destructive to your money. Switching off, removing the instrument and being
suspended all leave the wallet balance, the ledger and every completed top-up exactly as they
were.

## Availability

<Note>
  Automatic top-ups need recurring payments approved on the deployment's payment account.
  That is a separate approval from ordinary payments, granted separately, so a deployment can
  take one-off payments perfectly well and still not be able to charge a mandate. Where it has
  not been granted, the two calls that arm the arrangement (adding a payment method, and
  switching it on) answer `503 auto_topup_unavailable`. The panel still appears and still
  saves your three amounts, so they are ready the day it becomes available; it just says so
  and greys out the buttons that would arm it.

  **Manual top-ups always work regardless.** Nothing about automatic top-ups being unavailable,
  off, unfinished or suspended affects your ability to add money by hand, in the dashboard or
  from your own app.
</Note>

Your own backend can see where an account stands without anyone opening the dashboard.
`GET /v1/wallet` returns `autoTopUpState` and `autoTopUpThresholdPaise` next to the balance,
both in paise, and its `lowBalance` flag is measured against your threshold whenever an
arrangement is armed (`Active` or `AwaitingMandate`) and against the Rs 100 minimum top-up
otherwise, so every integration means the same thing by "low".
