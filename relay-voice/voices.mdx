A voice is chosen by alias. `GET /v1/relay/voice/voices` lists the aliases your key can use,
and the `voice` field on a send picks one of them.

```bash List the voices
curl https://api.tristack.tech/v1/relay/voice/voices \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY"
```

```json 200 OK
{
  "defaultVoice": "ravi",
  "pricePerCallPaise": 40,
  "voices": [
    { "voice": "asha", "name": "Asha (Hindi, female)", "language": "hi-IN" },
    { "voice": "ravi", "name": "Ravi (Indian English, male)", "language": "en-IN" }
  ]
}
```

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `voice` | The alias. This is the string you send. |
| `name` | A human label for a picker. Do not parse it. |
| `language` | What the voice was chosen for. A label, not a switch: see below. |
| `defaultVoice` | What you get when you send no `voice` at all. |
| `pricePerCallPaise` | 40 paise per answered call, the same for every voice. Voice choice never changes what a call costs. |

<Note>
  This endpoint runs the same product gate a send does, so a key whose project has Relay
  Voice switched off answers `403 relay_voice_disabled` here as well. It lists what you can
  actually order, never a menu you would be refused from.
</Note>

## Using one

```bash A named voice
curl https://api.tristack.tech/v1/relay/voice \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "to": ["+91XXXXXXXXXX"],
    "text": "Fees for term two are due on Friday. Please pay at the school office.",
    "voice": "asha"
  }'
```

The alias you used comes back on the response, and on every later read of the job, in the
`voice` field. Omit `voice` and you get `defaultVoice`, reported back the same way, so a
response always tells you which voice actually spoke.

An alias that is not on the list answers `400 unknown_voice`. A voice an operator has
switched off answers identically, because from a caller's side there is no difference between
a voice that never existed and one that is no longer selectable.

<Warning>
  `voice` cannot be sent alongside `audioId`. A stored recording already has a voice baked
  into it, and accepting a `voice` there would tell you that you chose something you did not
  get. Sending both is `400 invalid_request`.
</Warning>

## `language` is a label, not a switch

The `language` field says what a voice was picked for. It does not translate, transliterate,
or change how your text is read. What actually decides how the words come out is the voice
itself and the speech model behind it.

So `language` is useful for building a picker and for documenting your own choice, and it is
useless as an instruction. Send the text in the language you want spoken, and pick a voice
that suits it.

## Aliases, not provider ids

Which speech provider sits behind an alias, and what its internal voice id is, are
deliberately not exposed. The alias is the stable thing: it is what you store in your config,
what appears on your job records, and what keeps working if the engine underneath is
replaced. An opaque provider id in your codebase would be a hard dependency on a supplier you
never chose.

## Choosing a voice changes the recording

A recording is identified by its exact wording **and** its voice. The same sentence in two
voices is two recordings, two `audioId` values, and two synthesis charges of 3 paise per
character.

That is worth deciding early. Picking a voice, sending a hundred broadcasts, and then
switching voices means re-recording every one of those messages at full price. See
[reusing a recording](/relay-voice/reusing-audio) for what the id buys you and
[pricing](/relay-voice/pricing) for the arithmetic.

## Hear it before your customers do

The dashboard has two ways to audition a voice, and they answer different questions.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Play a sample in the browser" icon="play">
    Every voice in the picker has a play button. It plays a fixed sample sentence,
    synthesized in the **real** voice and speech model a job would use, not the provider's
    own marketing clip. That distinction matters: a marketing clip is a different engine's
    best take, and your recipients will not hear it.

    The sample is synthesized once per voice, ever, and served from storage afterwards. It
    costs you nothing, on any voice, however many times you press play.

    If the button is dead, the page will say so: speech is unconfigured on the deployment,
    and no send would work either.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Place a test call to a real phone" icon="phone">
    The dashboard can ring one number of your choosing and play that same sample in the
    voice you are auditioning. This is the only way to hear what a recipient hears: a voice
    through a mobile network's codec is not the voice through laptop speakers, and the phone
    is where the judgement matters.

    What it costs: **40 paise**, the normal price of one answered call. There is no synthesis
    charge, because the sample is our wording rather than yours and you are not being billed
    to prepare something you neither wrote nor keep. A real number rings and a real call is
    placed, so the call itself is charged like any other.

    It needs Relay Voice already switched on for the project you pick, since that is the
    project whose wallet pays and whose usage records it. The call shows up in your job
    history with `reference` set to `dashboard-test-call`.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Test-call yourself once per voice before a campaign, and once again after you change the
  wording of a message you care about. A sentence that reads cleanly can still land badly
  when spoken: numbers, abbreviations and long clauses are the usual culprits, and there is
  no SSML to fix them with. Rewriting the sentence is the fix.
</Tip>
