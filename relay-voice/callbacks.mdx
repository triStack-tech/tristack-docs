Polling works and costs nothing. A callback saves you from it: pass `callbackUrl` on the
send, and when the job finishes, Relay Voice POSTs the finished job to that URL.

```bash Ask to be told
curl https://api.tristack.tech/v1/relay/voice \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TRISTACK_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "to": ["+91XXXXXXXXXX", "+91YYYYYYYYYY"],
    "text": "Clinic closed tomorrow for Diwali. We reopen on Thursday at 9 am.",
    "callbackUrl": "https://your-app.example/hooks/relay-voice",
    "reference": "diwali-notice-2026"
  }'
```

It fires **once per job**, when every call in it has reached a terminal state. That includes a
job that ended as `Failed`: you are told either way, because a job that could not run is
exactly the thing you need to hear about.

<Warning>
  Delivery is **best effort**. One attempt, no retries, and we give up after about ten
  seconds. If your endpoint is down, deployed mid-flight, or slow, that notification is gone
  and nothing replays it.

  `GET /v1/relay/voice/{jobId}` is the authority on what happened. Treat the callback as a
  latency optimization over polling, never as the only path by which a job's outcome reaches
  your system.
</Warning>

## What is POSTed

The body is exactly the body of a job read. One representation of a job, whatever asks for
it, so you can point the same parser at both.

```json POST https://your-app.example/hooks/relay-voice
{
  "jobId": "rvj_cfa25cf49446fe8f8558de5c",
  "status": "Completed",
  "audioId": "aud_6a0c39fdf27efd2d9a0d70c0",
  "voice": "bella",
  "language": "en-IN",
  "recipients": 2,
  "estimatedCostPaise": 195,
  "costPaise": 195,
  "reference": "diwali-notice-2026",
  "error": null,
  "createdAt": "2026-08-21T18:30:00Z",
  "completedAt": "2026-08-21T18:31:47Z",
  "calls": [
    { "callId": "rvc_800aecacce6d294c6f4fe17c", "to": "+91XXXXXXXXXX", "status": "Completed",
      "attempts": 1, "durationSeconds": 11, "error": null, "costPaise": 0 },
    { "callId": "rvc_332316e97ffbd94ba0039bb9", "to": "+91YYYYYYYYYY", "status": "NoAnswer",
      "attempts": 1, "durationSeconds": null, "error": null, "costPaise": 0 }
  ]
}
```

<ResponseField name="jobId" type="string">
  The job this is about, `rvj_` plus 24 hex. Your key for deduplication.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="status" type="string">
  `Completed` or `Failed`. `Completed` means the dialing finished, **not** that anyone
  answered.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="error" type="string | null">
  Set only when `status` is `Failed`: `audio_unavailable`, `interrupted` (the service
  restarted mid-job; nothing is redialed, and since the calls were free there is nothing to refund), or
  `internal_error`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="costPaise" type="integer">
  What you were actually charged, in paise: the recording charge, if this job made the
  recording, and `0` if it replayed one. The calls are not metered, so this always equals
  `estimatedCostPaise`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="reference" type="string | null">
  Whatever you sent on the request. This is how you line the job up with your own records
  without keeping a `jobId` lookup table.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="calls" type="array">
  One entry per recipient, in the order you sent them, after deduplication.

  <Expandable title="call fields">
    <ResponseField name="callId" type="string">`rvc_` plus 24 hex.</ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="to" type="string">The number in E.164, as it was dialed.</ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="status" type="string">
      `Completed` (answered), `Busy`, `NoAnswer`, `Failed` or `Cancelled`.
    </ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="attempts" type="integer">Dial attempts made.</ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="durationSeconds" type="integer | null">Length of the answered call.</ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="error" type="string | null">A sentence about why this call did not connect.</ResponseField>
    <ResponseField name="costPaise" type="integer">`0`. Calls are not metered, in any outcome.</ResponseField>
  </Expandable>
</ResponseField>

Count `calls` where `status` is `Completed` to know how many people picked up. That is the
same count the billing uses.

## Headers

| Header | Value |
|---|---|
| `Content-Type` | `application/json; charset=utf-8` |
| `X-TriStack-Event` | `relay.voice.job.completed` |
| `X-TriStack-Signature` | `sha256=` followed by the lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256 of the exact request body |

Without the signature, a callback endpoint is an unauthenticated POST that anybody who learns
the URL can forge, and your system would act on call outcomes we never reported. Verify it on
every request, before you parse the body.

<Warning>
  **Per-customer signing secrets are not issued yet.** Do not email support asking for one:
  there is nothing to hand out, and the value the signature is currently keyed with is a
  server-side secret that will never be shared.

  Until a per-account secret ships, treat the signature as something you cannot verify, and
  do not rely on it as the only thing standing between your endpoint and a forged callback.
  Protect the endpoint another way: give every job a `callbackUrl` carrying a long random
  path segment or query token that only you and Tristack know, check that value on arrival,
  and treat the callback as a hint to re-read the job rather than as trusted data.

  The examples below are written against `TRISTACK_WEBHOOK_SECRET` so they are ready the day
  the secret exists. They will not verify against live callbacks before then.
</Warning>

<Note>
  **The safest pattern, available today.** Treat a callback purely as a signal that something
  changed, and get the facts from the API: on arrival, call
  `GET /v1/relay/voice/{jobId}` with your API key and use that response. A forged callback
  then costs you one authenticated read and can tell you nothing false.
</Note>

## Verifying the signature

The HMAC is computed over the **exact bytes we sent**. Parsing the JSON and re-serialising it
changes those bytes (key order, spacing, unicode escapes) and the signature will not match, so
capture the raw body first and parse it afterwards.

<CodeGroup>

```javascript Node (Express)
// Node 18 or newer. npm install express
import crypto from "node:crypto";
import express from "express";

const app = express();
const secret = process.env.TRISTACK_WEBHOOK_SECRET;

// express.raw, not express.json: the signature covers the bytes on the wire.
app.post("/hooks/relay-voice", express.raw({ type: "application/json" }), (req, res) => {
  const expected =
    "sha256=" + crypto.createHmac("sha256", secret).update(req.body).digest("hex");
  const received = req.get("X-TriStack-Signature") ?? "";

  // Same length first: timingSafeEqual throws on a length mismatch.
  const ok =
    expected.length === received.length &&
    crypto.timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(expected), Buffer.from(received));

  if (!ok) return res.status(401).send("bad signature");

  const job = JSON.parse(req.body.toString("utf8"));
  const answered = job.calls.filter((c) => c.status === "Completed").length;
  console.log(`${job.jobId}: ${answered}/${job.recipients} answered, ${job.costPaise} paise`);

  // Answer immediately; do the real work after. We wait about ten seconds, once.
  res.sendStatus(204);
});

app.listen(8080);
```

```python Python (Flask)
# pip install flask
import hashlib
import hmac
import os

from flask import Flask, request

app = Flask(__name__)
SECRET = os.environ["TRISTACK_WEBHOOK_SECRET"].encode()


@app.post("/hooks/relay-voice")
def relay_voice_hook():
    # get_data() is the raw body. request.json would re-parse and lose the bytes.
    body = request.get_data()
    expected = "sha256=" + hmac.new(SECRET, body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()

    if not hmac.compare_digest(expected, request.headers.get("X-TriStack-Signature", "")):
        return "bad signature", 401

    job = request.get_json()
    answered = sum(1 for c in job["calls"] if c["status"] == "Completed")
    app.logger.info(
        "%s: %d/%d answered, %d paise", job["jobId"], answered, job["recipients"], job["costPaise"]
    )
    return "", 204
```

```go Go (net/http)
package main

import (
	"crypto/hmac"
	"crypto/sha256"
	"encoding/hex"
	"encoding/json"
	"io"
	"log"
	"net/http"
	"os"
)

type call struct {
	Status string `json:"status"`
}

type job struct {
	JobID      string `json:"jobId"`
	Recipients int    `json:"recipients"`
	CostPaise  int64  `json:"costPaise"`
	Calls      []call `json:"calls"`
}

func main() {
	secret := []byte(os.Getenv("TRISTACK_WEBHOOK_SECRET"))

	http.HandleFunc("/hooks/relay-voice", func(w http.ResponseWriter, r *http.Request) {
		body, err := io.ReadAll(r.Body)
		if err != nil {
			http.Error(w, "unreadable body", http.StatusBadRequest)
			return
		}

		mac := hmac.New(sha256.New, secret)
		mac.Write(body)
		expected := "sha256=" + hex.EncodeToString(mac.Sum(nil))

		if !hmac.Equal([]byte(expected), []byte(r.Header.Get("X-TriStack-Signature"))) {
			http.Error(w, "bad signature", http.StatusUnauthorized)
			return
		}

		var j job
		if err := json.Unmarshal(body, &j); err != nil {
			http.Error(w, "bad json", http.StatusBadRequest)
			return
		}

		answered := 0
		for _, c := range j.Calls {
			if c.Status == "Completed" {
				answered++
			}
		}
		log.Printf("%s: %d/%d answered, %d paise", j.JobID, answered, j.Recipients, j.CostPaise)
		w.WriteHeader(http.StatusNoContent)
	})

	log.Fatal(http.ListenAndServe(":8080", nil))
}
```

</CodeGroup>

Compare in constant time, as all three do above. A byte-by-byte comparison that returns early
leaks, through its own timing, how much of a guessed signature was correct, and that is
enough to forge one.

## The URL rules, and why they exist

A `callbackUrl` is a destination our servers connect to, chosen by you. That makes the rules
around it security rules rather than style preferences.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="https only" icon="lock">
    An absolute `https://` URL, 2048 characters or fewer. Anything else is
    `400 invalid_callback_url` at send time, before the job is accepted.

    The body carries the phone numbers you called and what happened on each call. Over plain
    http, that is your customer contact list handed to everyone on the network path. There is
    no local exception and no development mode.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Redirects are not followed" icon="arrow-turn-down-right">
    We POST to the URL you gave us, and that is the only place the body goes. A `3xx` is
    recorded as a failed delivery, not followed.

    Following a redirect would hand the choice of destination back to the caller **after**
    the address was screened, which undoes the screening entirely. If your endpoint has
    moved, change the `callbackUrl` you send.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Private and loopback addresses are refused" icon="shield-halved">
    The destination is checked at the socket, when the connection is made: the host is
    resolved once, every address it answers with has to be publicly routable, and we connect
    to exactly those addresses.

    A URL pointing at `127.0.0.1`, a `10.x` address, or a cloud metadata endpoint would aim
    our egress, which sits inside our own network, at a target you chose. Resolving once and
    connecting to the resolved addresses is also what closes DNS rebinding: a hostname that
    is screened and then handed to an HTTP client gets resolved a second time, and whoever
    owns the record can answer publicly for the check and with `127.0.0.1` for the
    connection. There is no second lookup for anyone to race.

    In practice this means `https://localhost:3000/hooks` will never work, however you spell
    it. For local development, put a public https tunnel in front of your machine and use the
    tunnel's URL, or skip callbacks entirely and poll.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Building a receiver that holds up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify, then parse">
    Signature check on the raw bytes first, `401` if it fails, and only then treat the body
    as data.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Answer fast, work later">
    Return `2xx` as soon as you have stored the payload. We wait about ten seconds and do not
    retry, so a receiver that dials a database, sends emails and rebuilds a report before
    replying is a receiver that loses notifications.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Handle a jobId you have never seen">
    A single-recipient job can finish before your own `POST` call has returned and its `jobId`
    has been committed on your side. Store the payload keyed by `jobId` and reconcile, rather
    than rejecting what looks like an unknown job.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Be idempotent anyway">
    One delivery per job is the intent, not a guarantee you should build on. Key on `jobId`,
    make the second copy a no-op, and nothing downstream doubles up.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Poll for the jobs you were not told about">
    This is the step people skip. A sweep over your own jobs that have no recorded outcome
    after, say, an hour, reading `GET /v1/relay/voice/{jobId}` for each, is what turns best
    effort delivery into a system that always converges on the truth.
  </Step>
</Steps>
