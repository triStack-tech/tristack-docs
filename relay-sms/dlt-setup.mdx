You only need this page if your recipients must see **your** company name in the sender field.
With nothing set up, Relay SMS works immediately under Tristack's own registered header. See
[Relay SMS](/relay-sms/index).

<Note>
  Everything here happens on your operator's DLT portal, using your own login. Tristack cannot
  do it for you, and neither can anyone else: a DLT registration is issued to a legal entity
  after that entity is verified, and the portal has no API and no delegated access.
</Note>

## The four things you register

On any one operator's DLT portal (Jio, Airtel, Vi, BSNL or Tata):

1. **Your entity.** You are verified as a Principal Entity and given an entity ID.
2. **Your header.** Six alphabetic characters, related to your own name, brand or line of
   business. This is what the recipient sees.
3. **Your content template.** The exact text of the message, with `{#var#}` where the code
   goes. Operators match every message against this character for character.
4. **The telemarketer**, below. Without this last step your header is registered and your
   messages still will not deliver.

Registering an entity, a header and a template propagates to the other operators. **The
telemarketer binding does not.**

## Binding the telemarketer

Tristack's messages are delivered through a registered telemarketer, and your entity has to
authorise it before anything sent under your header will go out.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Telemarketer ID | `1702178720558766591` |
| Registered name | SID GROUPS PRIVATE LIMITED |

On Jio TrueConnect the path is **My Telemarketer → My SMS Telemarketer → New Chain**: search
for the ID, select it, and create the chain. It will sit at *pending* until it is approved
from the telemarketer's side, after which you return to **Pending Requests → Action → Approve**
to finish it. The other portals use different words for the same three steps.

<Warning>
  **Do this on every operator's portal whose subscribers you need to reach.** Entity, header
  and template registration propagates between operators; this binding does not.

  A customer who binds only Jio sees messages to Jio numbers arrive and messages to Airtel
  numbers fail, with no error on our side and nothing in the delivery report to explain it. It
  looks exactly like a fault at Tristack, and it is the single most common reason a
  bring-your-own-header setup half works.
</Warning>

## Writing the template

A few rules that are not obvious and that cause most rejections:

- The placeholder is written `{#var#}`, lower case. Some portals show it as `{#number#}`.
  Either is fine; what matters is that you paste the text **exactly as your portal shows it**.
- Your brand name must appear in the fixed text. It cannot be a variable.
- A variable cannot exceed 30 characters, and most of the message has to be fixed text.
- No double spaces, no curly quotation marks, and no link unless you have whitelisted it.
- Register it as **Service Implicit**. That is the category one-time codes belong in for a
  non-bank sender, and it reaches numbers registered on Do Not Disturb. A template registered
  as promotional will have most of its messages filtered.

A workable shape:

```text
{#var#} is your verification code for Acme. It is valid for 10 minutes. Do not share it with anyone. - Acme Fintech
```

<Note>
  If your template promises a validity, it has to be **ten minutes**, because that is how long
  a Relay SMS code lives. The text cannot be changed later without re-registering the template,
  so it is worth getting right the first time.
</Note>

## Putting it into Tristack

Open the project's **Relay SMS** tab and paste in four values: the entity ID, the registered
entity name, the header, the content template ID, and the approved text.

Paste the text rather than typing it. Every message is rendered from it and checked before
anything is charged, because an operator drops a message whose text does not match the
registration and that message is still billed. The screen shows you the finished sentence a
recipient will read, which is the quickest way to notice a wrong placeholder.

Then press **Start using my header**. Your messages switch to your own header on the next
send. There is no waiting period.

## What the statuses mean

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `draft` | Saved but not in use. Messages still go out under Tristack's header. |
| `ready` | In use. Your header is on every message from this project. |
| `verified` | Your template is now validated before each send, so a mismatch is refused rather than charged for. |
| `rejected` | Not accepted. Messages have fallen back to Tristack's header, so nothing has stopped working. The reason is shown on the tab. |
| `suspended` | Stopped. Messages have fallen back to Tristack's header. |

The fallback is deliberate. A problem with your registration should cost you your branding for
a while, not your ability to sign people in.

## When something does not deliver

| `code` | Means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dlt_sender_not_approved` | The header was refused. | Check spelling and letter case against your portal, and that the telemarketer binding is approved rather than pending. |
| `dlt_template_not_approved` | The template was refused. | The text in Tristack must match your registered template exactly. Re-copy it. |
| `dlt_blacklisted` | The header or template is blacklisted on DLT. | This is settled with your operator. Messages will keep failing until it is. |
| `dlt_content_rejected` | The operator refused the content. | Usually a link, an unexpected character, or a word on a blocked list. |

Editing and saving the registration again returns it to `draft`, because a changed header or
template is a different registration and the previous approval cannot carry across.

## Changing your mind

Remove the registration and messages go back to Tristack's header on the next send. Nothing
else changes: the same endpoints, the same keys, the same prices.
