---
title: "Relay SMS pricing"
description: "What a one-time code by SMS costs, what is free, and what is never charged twice."
icon: "indian-rupee-sign"
---

**40 paise per message sent.** One flat rate, whether the message goes out under your own
header or under Tristack's.

Charged from the same wallet as every other product, with no subscription, no minimum and no
per-project fee.

## What is not charged

- **Verifying.** Unlimited within the attempt cap, and free.
- **A resend inside the window.** Asking again for the same number within a minute returns the
  existing code and sends nothing, so nothing is charged. The response says `"reused": true`.
- **A refused send.** If the message is not accepted for delivery, the hold is released whole
  and you are not charged. `costPaise` in the response is what you actually paid.
- **A refusal before sending.** An invalid number, a rate limit, or a template that cannot be
  filled all cost nothing, because nothing was sent.

## What is charged even when it does not arrive

A message that is accepted and then dropped by the operator **is** charged. Delivery is
reported honestly rather than quietly refunded: `GET /v1/relay/sms/otp/{id}` shows
`delivery.status` as `failed` or `blocked`, and the usage view reports the cost of those
messages separately so it is visible rather than buried.

The commonest cause on your own header is a template mismatch, which is why Tristack checks the
rendered message against your registered text before spending anything. See
[Using your own header](/relay-sms/dlt-setup).

## Unlike Relay WhatsApp, there is no second bill

Relay WhatsApp charges 20 paise and Meta bills you separately for every conversation. Relay
SMS does not work that way: 40 paise is the whole cost of the message, and no operator or
aggregator will invoice you for anything.

If you bring your own DLT registration, your operator may charge you for the registration
itself. That is between you and them and does not reach your Tristack wallet.

## Seeing what you spent

Per-message lines appear in **Usage**, with the sender header as the item, so traffic on your
own header and traffic on Tristack's are told apart. The rate is also published at
`GET /api/v1/usage/rates`, so nothing has to hardcode it.
